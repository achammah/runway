Fair challenge. The fix to the law: divisions are never generated — they are engine state, born only from decisions you made in the game. The LLM names things and paints awnings; it never invents a division, a capacity or a dollar. Here is the whole mechanism:

2THE AXES ARE SCHEMA, THE NAMES ARE DRESSING — four axes exist in code; the DM only labels them in your world

"Sliced by ▾" offers ONLY the axes that have ≥2 divisions in state. A spa chain happens to populate site; a SaaS populates product; nobody decides this — the state does. The DM's whole role: when a site is born it proposes the name "Lyon"; when the works renders, it words the labels for your world ("studios", "plants", "categories").

THE SHARED ROW. What genuinely has no address — you, brand marketing, HQ rent — is NEVER smeared across divisions. It sits as one honest SHARED / HQ row under the slices. Direct vs allocated cost is a real lesson, so the game refuses to fake the allocation.
4WHY LYON ≠ GENEVA, MECHANICALLY — the comparison falls out of state, it is not written by anyone
A site carries: its rent (the quote you signed) · a local wage multiplier (set at open_site from the world table) · its own learning count (Lyon has done 4,100 sessions, Geneva 900) · its own demand ramp. Unit cost per site = the same formula, fed by that site's numbers. Geneva's $36 is not a story — it is Geneva's rent divided by Geneva's volume plus Geneva's wages.

✓ONE STORY, END TO END
Week 40, you write "open a second studio in Lyon." The clarify pass asks what it always asks (how big? who runs it?). The engine prices the lease from the world table, the DM proposes the name, THE PRE-ROLL REVIEW shows the capex and the hire pack, you sign. A site record is born with rent $2,100/wk, wage ×0.92, demand ramp week 1. From that Monday, every hire you route there, every table you buy it, every session it serves lands on Lyon's book because the records carry .site. Twelve weeks later the works shows Lyon at $27 a session — and nobody generated anything but the name.

So: schema-fixed axes, decision-born divisions, group-by books, one honest SHARED row, count-driven rungs — the LLM only names.
